# 11 - Direct hydrocarbon indicators (DHIs)

## Plain English
A DHI is something visible on seismic that is caused directly by hydrocarbons in the pores rather than by the rock type. They come straight out of notes 06-10:

- **Bright spot**: an unusually strong (usually negative) reflection from the top of a soft gas sand. Typical in young, shallow, poorly compacted basins (Class III).
- **Dim spot**: where the sand is *harder* than the shale (deeper, older rocks, Class I), gas *reduces* the positive reflection, so the anomaly is a weakening.
- **Polarity reversal**: where the sand is only slightly harder than shale (Class II), adding gas flips the reflection from positive to negative, possibly only at far offsets.
- **Flat spot**: a flat reflection from the **fluid contact** (gas-water, gas-oil or oil-water). Gravity makes contacts horizontal, so it cuts across dipping layers. It is one of the most convincing DHIs. Its reflection is positive, because the stiffer liquid lies under the gas.
- **Velocity sag / push-down**: slow gas makes reflections beneath it arrive late.
- **Attenuation shadow**: high frequencies are lost below gas (note 12).
- **Gas chimneys**: vertical zones of washed-out, disrupted seismic above leaking gas.
- **Amplitude conformance**: the anomaly's edge follows a structural contour, i.e. it stops at the spill point. This is a strong consistency check.

## Why they fail
- Low-saturation "fizz" gas gives the same bright spot as commercial gas (note 06).
- Coals, tight streaks, volcanics and processing artefacts can make bright or flat events.
- A flat spot needs the reservoir to be thicker than the vertical resolution, and must be checked in depth, not time, because velocity sag can make it look tilted or flat.
- Bright spots fade with depth: compaction makes sands and shales converge in impedance, and below the crossover the indicator becomes a dim spot.
- Oil is much harder to see than gas. Its modulus contrast with brine is smaller.

## Checkable facts
- A bright spot is a local amplitude increase associated with hydrocarbons, from the larger impedance contrast when hydrocarbons replace brine under shale. [source: wikipedia-bright-spot]
- Below a certain depth, sand and shale impedances cross over and dim spots become more useful than bright spots. [source: wikipedia-bright-spot]
- A flat spot can form where gas-filled porous rock overlies liquid-filled porous rock. The gas layer has lower impedance than the liquid layer. [source: wikipedia-flat-spot]
- A flat spot should be confirmed after depth conversion. [source: wikipedia-flat-spot]
- A flat spot requires a reservoir thicker than the vertical seismic resolution. [source: seg-wiki-dhi]
- Every hydrocarbon indicator can also be caused by non-hydrocarbon situations, so no single indicator is relied on alone. [source: seg-wiki-dhi]
- The Poseidon ML study used an AVO fluid factor and reported that a negative amplitude anomaly in it indicates gas. [source: arxiv-2410-21960-poseidon-ml]
